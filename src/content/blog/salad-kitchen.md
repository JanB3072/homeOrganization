The fastest way to understand a salad shop is to look at what people actually order. The Salad Kitchen's office catering menu on Feedr has a "most ordered" section, and it reads like a map of the brand. People's Champion, Protein Paradise, Gluten Go Home, the Crunchbox range and a Vegan Miso Brownie sit at the top. That list says more than a mission statement would.

This piece starts with the bestsellers, then works backwards to the company, the locations and the cooking approach. It is a London business, not a generic phrase for a place that sells salad, and I have kept the source of each fact clear because the official site and the ordering platforms show different things.

## The bowls people order most

Feedr describes itself as an office catering platform, and its page for The Salad Kitchen is marked with a 4.3 rating from 585 ratings. The "Popular items" section is described as a quick start with the most ordered items on the menu. It lists ten dishes, all priced in pounds before VAT.

Two patterns stand out. First, most of the bestsellers are single-serving bowls in a narrow ten to fourteen pound band. Second, the names are almost all promises rather than descriptions. People's Champion, Protein Paradise and Founders' Fave tell you how the bowl is meant to feel, not what is in it. That is a deliberate style, and it works best when the brand already has regulars.

The sharing bowl is the anomaly. At £101.55 for six, it is the most expensive item on the popular list and the only one sized for a group. Its presence suggests that office orders are a serious part of the business.

## What the rest of the menu looks like

The same Feedr page divides the menu into Our Favourites, Crunch boxes, Platters, Smoothies and Drinks, Add ons, Dressings, Sweet Treats and Bespoke Buffets. There are twelve items in Our Favourites, twelve Crunch boxes, six Platters and nine drinks. That is a manageable menu for a shop that builds bowls to order.

The catering side reaches further. The Bespoke Buffets section includes a Build Your Own Salad Bowl Pop-Up priced at £1,150 for a hundred people, and versions with dessert or larger portions at £1,500 and £1,600. Those are not ordinary takeaway prices. They are event prices, and they confirm that the company sells a service as well as lunch.

Delivery listings show a slightly different menu. Deliveroo pages for the Welbeck Street and Leather Lane branches mention Leaf Box regular and large, BYO bundles, Pulled Chicken Pots and focaccia sandwiches. Those items do not appear in the Feedr popular list, which is a reminder that a menu depends on the channel. A bowl ordered at a desk through an office platform is not always the same bowl ordered at lunch through an app.

## The company behind the bowls

The official site calls itself London's favourite salad shop and lists branches in Mayfair, Farringdon, Old Street, Royal Exchange, Mansion House and Canary Wharf. All of those are central London or financial district locations, which fits the office catering emphasis. A directory listing gives an address at 126 Whitecross Street, and Deliveroo lists branches on Welbeck Street and Leather Lane. The street names differ because the shops are in different parts of the city.

The brand's description is short and direct. It wants to create salads that are more than sideshows to the main event, as satisfying and indulgent as a guilty treat. Its homepage adds that the bowls are built to order and that every element has been roasted, fermented or marinated in house. Those are preparation claims from the company. They are not independently verified, and they matter because they explain why a salad here can cost more than a supermarket one.

The menu page says salad bases change each week. A March entry lists couscous with salsa verde, black beans with muhammara, pickled red cabbage, raw slaw in miso and sesame dressing, mixed green leaves and roast new potatoes with maple, thyme and Dijon. The same page lists allergens for each base, including gluten, mustard, sulphites, soya and sesame. If you have an allergy, the weekly change is the detail that matters.

## Why these bowls sell

The bestseller list is not random. It answers the three questions people ask at a salad counter: will this fill me up, what does it taste like, and is it safe for my diet. Protein Paradise answers the first. Founders' Fave and People's Champion answer the second with a brand story. Gluten Go Home answers the third. So Cali is marked low calorie, and the Vegan Miso Brownie covers dessert.

That is a well-built menu. It does not try to be everything. It offers a set of named combinations, then lets the customer move between them with a few clear labels.

## The home version of a built-to-order bowl

A bowl built to order has a base, a protein, vegetables and a dressing. That is also a useful way to think about the contents of a home fridge. If you can see a base, a protein and two vegetables, lunch is five minutes away. If everything is in opaque containers at the back, it is a search.

## The honest summary

The Salad Kitchen is a London salad chain with a clear idea: make salads substantial enough to be lunch and give the bowls names people remember. Its bestsellers on the office catering platform are People's Champion, Protein Paradise and several Crunchbox variations, all in a similar price band. It also handles catering for offices and events.

If you are ordering, start with the most-ordered list and check the weekly bases for allergens. If you are cooking, borrow the structure rather than the names. A base, a protein, a vegetable and a sauce, all visible and within reach, will take you further than a long recipe collection.
