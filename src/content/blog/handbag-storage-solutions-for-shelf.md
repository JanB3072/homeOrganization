Before buying anything, count the bags and divide. That sounds like the least interesting advice in closet organization and it is also the step that decided every purchase I made on this shelf. Nine bags, one 36 inch shelf, and a number I had never calculated: usable width divided by the widest tote plus half an inch of clearance.

The answer was three. Not five, which is what the shelf looked like it could hold at a glance, and not nine, which is what it had been holding. Everything I bought afterwards followed from that one division, and the two systems that survived are the ones that respect it.

## Divide the shelf before you shop

Bag widths are not close to each other, which is why the average is useless.

| Bag type | Typical width | Fits on a 36 in shelf | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clutch | 8 to 9 in | 3, with room left over | Better stacked flat than standing |
| Shoulder bag | 10 to 12 in | 3 | The most common size and the awkward one |
| Structured tote | 13 to 16 in | 2 | Sets the cell width for the whole run |
| Weekender | 18 to 20 in | 1 | Usually belongs on the floor or a low shelf |

Add half an inch between bags and a fifth left empty, and a 36 inch shelf holding nine is honestly a three bag shelf. The other six are reclassified: clutches flat in a box, the weekender low, the daily bags on the eye level row.

## Pick the system by the problem, not the product

This is where most shelf money gets wasted. Three systems solve three different problems, and buying the wrong one leaves the actual damage untouched.

| System | Solves | Does not solve | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acrylic cells | Slouching, contact and colour transfer | Dust, humidity | about $28 for six cells |
| Purse pillows | Internal collapse, creasing | Contact between bags | $6 to $10 each, or a rolled towel |
| Cotton dust bags | Dust and light | Shape, contact | $2 to $4 each |

The [acrylic divider setup](/blog/acrylic-purse-dividers-for-closet-shelves-organizer/) is where I started, and the reason it worked is that it attacked the problem that was actually ruining bags, which was contact and lean rather than dust. Divider cells are mechanical: they stop one bag touching another and hold each one vertical. They do nothing for a bag sitting in a draught of humid air.

## Purse pillows are the most misused item on this shelf

A pillow is not packing material. It is a shape support, and the difference shows up in how full you make it.

Leather has a memory in both directions. Under-filled, a tote folds along the base and keeps that crease. Over-filled, the panels bow outward, the seams take the strain, and patent finishes stretch in a way that never recovers. The working range is 80 to 85 percent, where the panels are supported but the profile still looks like the bag.

What goes inside matters as much. Newspaper, ordinary tissue and paper bags are acidic, and over a season they yellow the lining. Acid-free tissue runs about eight dollars a pack, and a rolled cotton towel is the free alternative for totes as long as it is clean and dry.

## Dust bags have a direction problem

A dust bag protects against dust and light. It also blocks airflow if it is made of the wrong material, and the wrong material is most of what ships free with a bag.

- Cotton and unlined non-woven breathe. Safe for leather that went in dry.
- Laminated non-woven and plastic pouches seal. A bag that came in from the rain will smell of a gym bag by spring.
- Dark unlined non-woven transfers dye onto pale leather. Wash it once, or use a white cotton pillowcase.

Daily bags live uncovered, weekly bags get cotton, seasonal bags get cotton plus a position away from the exterior wall. A shelf where every bag is wrapped is a shelf you stop using.

## Height assignment is a lifting problem

The shelf that photographs best has every bag at eye level. The shelf that works is assigned by weight.

Structured dailies go at chest height, where the lift is short. Heavy totes go low, because a 3 pound bag lifted from above shoulder height with one arm is how shoulders get hurt. Clutches and anything without structure go on top, flat, with tissue between layers.

Soft bags never go in cells. A slouchy hobo has nothing for a divider to brace, and forcing one in distorts it while wasting a cell. They live flat in [stackable clear boxes](/blog/clear-plastic-shoe-boxes-stackable-design/), the same method that keeps the sneakers visible, and the shelf keeps its row of structured bags.

The row is only one layer of the closet. ## The shelf after a year

Nine bags became six, then five. Two left because the division proved they would never fit a real row, and two more because a year of not carrying them answered a question I had been avoiding.

The cost was about fifty dollars: cells, tissue and two pillows. The bags that stayed are upright, unstained and flat on their bases. The one I regret is the tote I hung by its handles for two years, because the straps are an inch longer than they should be and no shelf fixes that.
