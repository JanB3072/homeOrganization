Three words, four unrelated answers. I typed "lucie kitchen" into a search bar to see what would come back, and the first screen gave me a Michelin-recommended dining room on Yonge Street, a caterer in Sydney, a cake book, and a woman in Amsterdam who organizes other people's kitchens for a living. Nothing was wrong with any of the results. The problem was that they all claimed the same three words.

This is one of those queries where the search engine is doing its job and the reader still leaves empty handed. There is no single thing called Lucie Kitchen. There are several real things, each perfectly legible once you know which one you meant. Below is the map, the spelling trap that makes it worse, and the practical version for anyone whose actual problem is a small kitchen rather than a name.

## Four things answer to the name

| Result on screen | What it really is | Where it lives | The noun that gives it away |
|---|---|---|---|
| Restaurant Lucie | Modern French dining room, Michelin-recommended | 100 Yonge Street, Toronto | Chef, reservation, tasting menu |
| Your SPACE by Lucie | Professional organizing and decluttering practice | Amsterdam, Netherlands | Categories, virtual session, intake call |
| Lucie's Gourmet Kitchen | Caterer doing buffets and grazing platters | Sydney, Australia | Delivery, platter, ABN |
| fromlucie.com | Baking recipes and a cookbook announced for spring 2026 | Online | Loaf, cake, butter |

A fifth entry keeps sneaking in: Lucie Rhéaume's kitchen home tour, a widely shared photo spread with an itemised budget. It is a real kitchen belonging to a real person, not a brand. Search engines love it because the words sit together in the title. Readers sometimes arrive expecting a product and leave with a cabinet quote.

## The restaurant owns the exact phrase

Of all four, only one puts the words in the order you typed them. Restaurant Lucie's own about page says, flatly, "Lucie's kitchen is led by Chef Michael Angeloni." If your search came from a menu, a reservation or a dinner conversation, this is almost certainly what you were after, and the phrase you found is the restaurant describing itself.

The facts are easy to pin down. The dining room sits at 100 Yonge Street in Toronto's Financial District. It runs on modern French technique, ingredient-led and deliberate. The partners are Yannick Bigourdan, Executive Chef Michael Angeloni and Adam Teolis. The name reaches back to a grandmother named Lucie who lived near the Spanish border in the Pyrenees, and the site frames the restaurant as an echo of her belief that feeding someone well is the most important thing you can do for them. Now Toronto, quoted on that page, calls it Michelin-recommended.

None of that has anything to do with storage bins. It matters here because it explains why the query behaves the way it does. The restaurant publishes the exact phrase, so search engines hand it the exact phrase. Everything else in this article is a near match fighting for the same slot.

## The organizer version, which is the one worth stealing from

The second Lucie is Lucie Krobova, founder of Your SPACE by Lucie in Amsterdam. Her site calls her a professional organizer and a Chronic Disorganization Specialist. She completed intensive training in autumn 2023 to become a Certified Virtual Organizing Professional, has worked in close to 200 houses, and spent four years as an au pair for a family before she started organizing professionally. Her son Samuel arrived in August 2025, which shows up in the way she writes about timelines and interrupted days.

Her method starts with a category pass before any decluttering happens. Kitchen is one of those categories, and she defines it with unusual precision: food, food-prep, food-serving, food storage, and kitchen paraphernalia. That split is the useful part. Most small-kitchen advice treats a drawer as a drawer. Hers treats the drawer as one of four jobs, and the jobs do not mix well.

In my 580-square-foot apartment, that distinction changed how the cabinets got assigned. Food and food storage are quiet, static things: jars, tins, grains, the back of a shelf. Food-prep is the zone that gets wet and dirty, so it belongs near the sink and near a surface you can wipe. Food-serving wants to be where you actually set a plate down, which in a studio is often three feet from the cabinet it came out of. Kitchen paraphernalia is the catch-all that quietly eats the most space, and it is the only category that deserves a hard limit.

The category order she publishes is clothing, books, bathroom, then kitchen. That sequencing is worth noticing. Kitchen comes after the easy wins, not before them, because a kitchen full of decisions is a bad place to start when your energy is already low. The same logic sits behind the [decluttering guide for overwhelmed days](/blog/how-to-start-decluttering-when-overwhelmed/), which insists on one surface rather than one room.

## The other two, so you can stop scrolling

Lucie's Gourmet Kitchen is a Sydney caterer. The NSW government supplier register lists the business name and ABN 86 647 752 184, and describes menus made to order, including buffet and grazing options. Restaurant listings add beef shawarma, kafta skewers and veggie burgers with house-made patties. This is a food business, not a kitchen you can visit and not a product you can buy off a shelf.

The other Lucie is a baker. Lucie Franc de Ferriere shared a tahini, black sesame and Earl Grey marble loaf with Seed + Mill, and her book Cake by Lucie was announced for spring 2026 through fromlucie.com. The description there says her bakes are less sweet than American-style baked goods, which is a real point of view rather than a marketing line. If the result you clicked mentioned a loaf tin, you are in this lane.

## A five-line filter that beats another search

When the same three words keep returning different worlds, the fastest fix is not a better query. It is a quick sort based on the nouns on the screen.

1. Does the page say chef, reservation, tasting menu or an address with a street number? Restaurant. Close the tab if you wanted a shelf.
2. Does it say delivery, buffet, grazing platter or a business number? Caterer. Useful for a party, useless for a pantry.
3. Does it say category, session, intake call or declutter? Organizer. This is the one with advice you can use at home.
4. Does it say loaf, cake, butter or cookbook? Baker. You will leave hungry, but informed.
5. Does the name contain Lucy with a y? Different person. Lucy in the Kitchen and Lucy's Kitchen Show are recipe sites, and Lucie Rhéaume's home tour is a separate thing again. Start over.

That filter takes about ten seconds. It saves the afternoon you would otherwise spend clicking between a French tasting menu and a cake recipe, wondering where the storage tips went.

## What the four-part split looks like in a small kitchen

Here is where the organizer's category idea earns its keep. I mapped her four jobs onto the cabinet space I actually have, and the result is less a system than a set of rules about what is allowed where.

| Job | What lives there | Where it goes in 580 square feet | The mistake it prevents |
|---|---|---|---|
| Food | Dry goods, tins, unopened jars | Upper shelf, back, out of the light | Groceries drifting into the prep zone and never coming back |
| Food-prep | Cutting boards, knives, mixing bowls | Drawer and shelf nearest the sink | Wet tools migrating into clean storage |
| Food-serving | Plates, cups, napkins | Within arm's reach of the one eating surface | Stacking dishes in a cabinet across the room |
| Food storage | Containers, labels, wraps | One bin, not four drawers | Lids separating from their boxes forever |
| Paraphernalia | Gadgets, spares, odd bits | One hard-limited bin, reviewed monthly | The slow takeover of every free surface |

Two of those rows needed actual hardware. The prep zone only worked after the [deep cabinet organizers](/blog/how-to-organize-deep-kitchen-cabinets/) stopped hiding bowls at the back, and the serving row stopped failing once the [pull-out shelf guide](/blog/diy-pull-out-shelves-for-kitchen-cabinets/) gave the everyday plates a home that slides out instead of being dug through. For the storage bin itself, the [cabinet hub](/kitchen/cabinets/) has the measurements I keep reusing, and the [pan organizer rack](/blog/best-pan-organizer-rack-for-kitchen-cabinet/) handles the one category that refuses to lie flat.

The paraphernalia bin is the honest weak point. A hard limit sounds organised until a new gadget arrives, and then the bin quietly expands. The rule I landed on is one in, one out, checked on the same day I unpack anything new. That is the [20/20 rule](/blog/20-20-decluttering-rule-explained/) applied to a single bin rather than a whole house, which is a much easier promise to keep.

## The failure case worth naming

The expensive mistake here is borrowing from the wrong Lucie. A restaurant kitchen is built for volume, with stainless shelving, open racks and equipment within reach of a line cook. A 580-square-foot apartment kitchen has roughly ten usable inches of cabinet depth in places and a door swing that eats the rest. Copy the restaurant and you buy a rack that does not fit, then keep it because returning it feels like admitting the search was pointless.

The same thing happens with the home-tour version. The Lucie Rhéaume kitchen that circulates online has custom fronts quoted at $12,973 against IKEA cabinet structure at $3,385, a combined $16,358 before the countertop and installation. Those numbers describe a renovation, not a storage problem. Browsing it for ideas is fine. Treating it as the benchmark for a rental kitchen is how a simple drawer problem turns into a spending problem.

What transfers between all four Lucies is much smaller and much cheaper: name the job, give it one place, keep the places unequal in size because the jobs are unequal. The [small pantry hacks](/blog/small-pantry-cabinet-organization-hacks-for-apartment/) use that same move on dry goods, and the [studio kitchen shelves](/blog/floating-shelves-storage-ideas-for-tiny-studio-kitchen/) show what happens when the wall has to do the work the cabinet cannot.
