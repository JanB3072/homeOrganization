Start with the food, because that is what the name fails to explain. Bushfire Kitchen is a Southern California fast-casual chain, and its menu is built around bowls, grilled plates, burgers, sandwiches, salads and empanadas. The word bushfire is a brand name, not a cooking method and not a warning about the food.

I am writing this from the chain's own pages plus review and delivery listings, and I will be clear about which is which. The official site gives the positioning and the categories. The specific dish names and prices mostly come from menu platforms, which are useful but can lag behind.

## The menu, before anything else

The Riverside location page lists the categories plainly: bowls, plates, burgers, sandwiches, salads, hot sides, empanadas, and craft beer and wine. The same page says the kitchen uses fresh, local produce, offers both healthy and indulgent options, and provides gluten-free and plant-based choices. That combination explains the brand better than the slogan does.

Review and delivery pages fill in the dishes. The Cage-Free Chicken Bowl appears as a bestseller at several branches, usually around 15 to 16 dollars and marked gluten-free. The Thai Chicken Curry Bowl is listed near 17 to 18 dollars. A Grass-fed Tri-Tip Bowl appears around 18, and the En Fuego Burger and Bushfire Burger sit around 15 to 18. The Gaucho Steak Sandwich and Tuscan Chicken Sandwich are named repeatedly in "popular dishes" lists.

One delivery listing describes the Bushfire Burger as a half pound Colorado grass-fed Angus patty with cheddar, lettuce, pickles, red onion and house-made peri mayo. The California Burger adds avocado, arugula, tomato and basil pesto aioli. These are third-party descriptions, so treat them as a picture of the menu rather than a guarantee of the current recipe.

The ordering platform describes the food as comfort food that redefines classic American cuisine while accommodating vegan, vegetarian and gluten-free preferences. Its own page rendered as a loading shell when I opened it, so I could not confirm prices there. The menu platform figures are the best available public numbers.

## What the brand says about itself

The homepage calls Bushfire Kitchen "Fast Healthy Casual". The Riverside page adds the founding year, 2012, and a mission of healthy, delicious food at affordable prices. The chain also runs catering for parties from eight to 350 or more people, sells gift cards and maintains an active social media presence.

Four words sum up the positioning: fast, healthy, casual, affordable. Each of those has a practical meaning. Fast means counter service and a 11am to 9pm day. Healthy means a menu with grilled bowls and gluten-free markings, not a diet clinic. Casual means you order at the counter and choose your own table. Affordable in this context means a main dish in the mid teens, which is not cheap in an absolute sense but is reasonable for a sit-down fast-casual meal in Southern California.

This is useful because it sets expectations. If you want a burger with a patio and a beer, the menu has that, and the [kitchen table guide](/blog/desk-organization-small-apartment-kitchen-table/) makes a similar point about one table serving several roles at home. If you want a grain bowl with grilled chicken and no gluten, the menu has that too. The same kitchen is trying to serve both, and that is why the "healthy" label should be read as an option rather than a rule.

## Where it is

The official site has a location finder, and individual pages are indexed. The ones I could verify are Riverside at 5225 Canyon Crest Drive, Suite 92, phone 951-534-0697; Long Beach at 4610 Pacific Coast Highway, phone 562-342-4134, described as the first LA County location; Mission Viejo at 24420 Alicia Parkway; and Temecula at 40665 Winchester Road. Rancho Cucamonga appears on review sites with a long history of customer reviews but did not show up in the official pages I opened.

The pattern is worth naming. These are suburban Southern California locations, often in shopping centres with patios, and the [pan organizer guide](/blog/best-pan-organizer-rack-for-kitchen-cabinet/) is a useful reminder that a place working well is usually about a sensible layout more than a big floor plan. Riverside sits in Canyon Crest Towne Centre and mentions umbrellas and heaters on the patio. Long Beach is in Circle Marina Center near the Traffic Circle. This is a regional chain, not a national one, and the address is what determines whether you can actually go.

Hours at several branches are listed as 11am to 9pm daily. Riverside adds a happy hour from 2pm to 5pm. Those details change, so the location page or a phone call is the final word.

## Why the "healthy" claim needs a second look

A restaurant can offer healthy choices without being a healthy restaurant. Bushfire Kitchen does both. The bowls with grilled chicken, salmon or tri-tip and marked gluten-free status are one side of the menu. The burgers with cheese, bacon-style toppings and house sauces are the other.

This is not a criticism. It is a way to read the menu honestly. The chain says it uses fresh local produce and accommodates plant-based and gluten-free diets. Those are real options if you choose them. They do not turn a burger with peri mayo into a health food.

The practical approach is to decide what you want before you reach the counter, just as you decide which [airtight container](/blog/best-airtight-food-storage-containers-for-flour-and-sugar/) suits an ingredient before filling the cupboard. If it is a bowl, look for the protein and the sauce. If it is a burger, accept that it is a burger and enjoy it. The kitchen is not hiding anything; the word healthy in the brand name is simply broader than the word might suggest.
